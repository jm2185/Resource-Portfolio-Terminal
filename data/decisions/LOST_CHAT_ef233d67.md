# Idea 1 — Bull/Bear adversarial layer inside the pipeline

## What it is
Your chain is **scout → synthesis → verifier** — find-then-red-team. You insert a structured **debate** between synthesis and verifier: a **Bull** agent builds the strongest possible long case, a **Bear** builds the strongest short/pass case, each forced to ground every claim in *your* engine numbers, then a thin reconciler (or synthesis itself) weighs the exchange before verifier gates. This is the single most-copied finding from the TradingAgents work — two advocates beat one model "weighing both sides," because adversarial pressure surfaces the disconfirming evidence a single pass rationalizes away.

The point isn't drama. In a **4-name concentrated book**, the downside narrative is worth more *before* you size in than after — there's no diversification to wash out a bad underwrite. The debate forces the bear case onto the desk as a visible artifact, not a footnote.

## Why your engine makes this unusually good
Your debate isn't generic "is the stock good" — it's scoped to the exact axes `asymmetry_rating.py` already computes, which means **the engine can referee the argument**:
- **Bull must clear the JSF gate.** A long thesis that only works if you ignore a forensic cap (`_forensic_gate`, cap ≈4–4.5) is auto-killed by your own min-gate — visibly, with the reason string.
- **Bear attacks φ (floor coverage) and ρ (payoff ratio).** These are single numbers (`pillars.V.floor_coverage`, `pillars.V.rho`). "Your 3:1 payoff assumes the bull target; at the base leg ρ collapses to 1.2" is a *checkable* claim, not vibes.
- **The directive is the scoreboard.** `BELOW FLOOR — ACCUMULATE` vs `UPSIDE SPENT — HOLD/TRIM` already encodes the verdict the debate is arguing toward.

## Sub-features

**① Surface the asymmetry metrics into agent-facing state** — `PREREQUISITE` · *seam: `core.py:761` `get_conviction_ratings`* · **low effort, highest leverage**
This is the real first step. Widen the basket projection to include `pillars.V` (ρ, payoff, floor_coverage, upside_pct, downside_to_floor_pct), `gate` (cap + reason), `confidence_ribbon`, and the price `ladder`. *Until the Bear can read the number it's attacking, the debate is vapor.* Bonus: fixes the latent `T/Q/V` selection bug while you're in there.

**② Engine-refereed claims (the "fact gate")** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: new agent files + reconciler step* · **medium**
Each debater must tag claims as *engine-grounded* (cites a live field) vs *narrative* (web/judgment). The reconciler weights grounded claims higher and flags any narrative claim that contradicts an engine field. Turns "trust me" into "the floor says otherwise."

**③ Live debate in the PIPELINE panel** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: `pipeline_event(stage=…)` → `commodityex_tui.py:987`* · **low**
Post `bull` / `bear` / `reconcile` as stages so the desk watches rounds land in real time. The losing side's strongest point survives as a `highlight_ticker(level="warn")` badge — dissent doesn't evaporate when the bull wins.

**④ Convergence / confidence score** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: reconciler output* · **low–medium**
Don't just pick a winner — emit *how close* it was. A name where bull and bear both land near the same directive is high-confidence; a 51/49 split flows to verifier flagged "contested," and contested names get a tighter verifier pass. Calibrated disagreement is signal.

**⑤ Asymmetric debate weighting by archetype** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: agent prompts read `archetype`* · **low**
For `option_convexity` (the spear), bias the debate toward V/asymmetry — that's where the edge and the risk are. For `asset_light_yield` royalties, bias toward Q/cash-flow durability and let the bear attack accretion quality, not dilution (which your gate already exempts). The debate should argue about what actually matters *for that archetype*.

**⑥ Steelman handoff** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: reconciler → synthesis* · **low**
The winning side must restate the *loser's* best argument before synthesis proceeds (the Rapoport rule). Cheap insurance against motivated reasoning; reads beautifully in the dossier.

**Effort overall:** Medium. Two `.claude/agents/` files + one orchestration step in `pipeline.md`, reusing every existing tool. **Sub-feature ① is the only thing that touches engine/MCP code — and it's the unlock for everything else here.**

# Idea 2 — Calibration / decision-journal loop

## What it is
You persist dossiers (`data/decisions/*.md`) and pipeline verdicts, but **nothing ever scores them against reality**. A `@calibration` agent that, on a horizon, grades each past APPROVE / CONDITIONAL / REJECT and each engine `upside_pct` / floor estimate against what actually happened — *"your APPROVEs hit 7/10; your bull on AGA.V projected 138%, realized 41%; your REP floors ran 12% conservative on average."* This is the superforecaster discipline: you cannot fix overconfidence without scored feedback. Your own engine map named *"no backtest / reconciliation"* as a real gap — this closes it.

The payoff loop: calibration → evidence → `propose_param_change("conservatism_scalar", …)` → your existing `/confirm` governance. Realized outcomes flow back into the valuation through the human-gated path you already built (`core.py:642` propose, `661` confirm). It turns your dossier archive from **write-only** into a **learning system**.

## Why concentration makes this high-leverage
With four names you make *few* decisions, so you can actually track each one — and systematic bias (always 15% too bullish on upside, or floors always too conservative) is a real money error rather than statistical noise. The base rate it produces becomes a prior every future scout/synthesis run inherits.

## The two decisions that make it real (not aspirational)

**The ground-truth source and horizon — name them or the idea is hand-waving:**
- **Source:** realized price from `fmp_client.py` (`quote` / historical) — you already have the client and cache.
- **Horizon:** measure decision-date → **30 / 90 / 180d**, scored against *which leg* — floor, base, bull, bear. A bull call is "right" if price approached the bull leg in the window; a floor is "right" if it held. Multi-horizon because a discovery thesis and a royalty re-rate play out on different clocks.

## Sub-features

**① Structured decision records** — `PREREQUISITE` · *seam: `_save_decision` `commodityex_tui.py:1584` + `engine.py:3197`* · **low–medium**
Current dossiers are plain markdown, no frontmatter (confirmed) — **you can't grade what isn't machine-readable.** Add YAML frontmatter or a sidecar `decisions/index.jsonl`: `ticker, date, verdict, price_at_decision, floor/base/bull/bear legs, ρ, φ, JSF`. This is the foundation; calibration is impossible without it. Cheap, and it makes the dossier library queryable for everything else too.

**② Outcome capture tool** — `PREREQUISITE` · *seam: new MCP tool `record_outcome` in `core.py`/`server.py`* · **medium**
The 1:1 wrapper pattern (`@mcp.tool()` → `core.fn`) makes this mechanical. Stamps realized price at each horizon against the frozen legs. Can run on a `/loop` or schedule so outcomes accrue without you remembering.

**③ The Brier / calibration scorecard** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: `@calibration` agent + `/journal` skill* · **medium**
Hit rate by verdict class, mean error on `upside_pct`, floor conservatism bias, and a reliability curve ("when you said 70% confident, you were right 55% of the time"). One screen. This is the artifact you'd actually look at monthly.

**④ Evidence-backed `propose_param_change`** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: `core.py:642` → `/confirm`* · **low once ③ exists**
"Floors ran 12% conservative across 8 closed decisions → propose `conservatism_scalar` 0.88 → 0.92." Routes through your existing human gate — the agent never sets, only proposes with receipts. *This is the compounding payoff: the engine literally learns from being wrong.*

**⑤ Per-archetype calibration** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: scorecard groups by `archetype`* · **low**
You're probably well-calibrated on royalties and wildly optimistic on explorers (or vice versa). Splitting the scorecard by archetype tells the bull/bear layer (Idea 1) *exactly where to apply extra skepticism* — the two ideas compound. Explorer upside calls running hot → bear gets a heavier thumb on the spear.

**⑥ Pre-mortem base rates fed forward** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: scout/synthesis prompt injection* · **low**
Before a new APPROVE, inject the relevant base rate: *"Your last 6 sub-$50M explorer APPROVEs hit 2/6 at the 90d bull leg."* Turns history into a live prior at decision time, not a quarterly retrospective.

**⑦ Decision-quality vs outcome-quality split** — `ENHANCEMENT` · *seam: scorecard* · **low**
Grade the *process* separately from the *result* — a well-reasoned call that lost to a macro shock isn't a process failure. Prevents you from over-fitting the engine to noise (the classic resulting fallacy). The most intellectually honest sub-feature here.

**Effort overall:** Medium–high — it needs the new tool + an outcome store — but it's the **highest-compounding** idea you have. ①②④ are the load-bearing trio; ③⑤⑥⑦ are where it gets genuinely smart.

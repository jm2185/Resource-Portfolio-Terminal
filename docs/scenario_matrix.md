First, the four scenarios themselves, with your probability weights:
Debasement (40%) — the base case. Currency is gradually inflated away, real rates stay negative, the government runs hot deficits but in an orderly way. Hard assets and the things that can't be printed win; cash and bonds quietly lose purchasing power.
Fiscal crisis (15%) — the disorderly tail. The same debasement pressure, but it breaks violently: a bond-market dislocation, credit stress, flight to safety. This is the scenario where the system strains, not just the currency.
AI upside (30%) — productivity actually shows up. Real growth is strong, real rates can stay high or rise, and the monetary debasement trade cools because the economy is genuinely creating value rather than just printing it. Power demand surges.
Benign (15%) — the boring soft landing. Rates normalize, growth is fine, no debasement, no crisis. Nothing dramatic happens, and ordinary productive businesses just compound.
Now each sleeve, read across all four:
Silver spear (AGA.V) — wins / wins / lags / lags. Your highest-torque monetary bet. It rips in both debasement and crisis because silver is the leveraged expression of "money is being debased." But in the AI-upside world it lags (real rates up, no monetary panic, a pre-revenue junior has no catalyst), and in the benign world it lags too (no debasement bid, nothing to move it). Strong on the left half of the board, dead on the right.
Royalty / producer — wins / wins / flat / flat. The same left-side strength as the spear but softer, because royalties throw off cash. They win in both monetary scenarios, and in the AI and benign columns they don't win but they don't break either — the cash flow holds them flat rather than letting them bleed.
Uranium — flat / flat / wins / flat. The odd one out, and deliberately so. It doesn't really care about the monetary scenarios (its own supply-deficit cycle drives it), so it's flat in debasement and crisis. Its one clean win is AI upside — that's the whole reason it's in the book, to cover the column your metals can't. Flat in benign.
Quality compounders — flat / flat / wins / wins. The mirror image of the spear. They don't need debasement, so they're flat-to-neutral on the left half. But in AI upside they win big (pricing power, reinvestment, productivity tailwind), and in benign they win too (a calm compounding world is exactly their habitat). They fill the right half of the board that your current book leaves empty.
Financials / rates — wins / lags / wins / wins. The widest-covering sleeve, with one sharp hole. They win in debasement (a steeper curve fattens net interest margins), win in AI upside (strong growth, higher rates), and win in benign (healthy economy, normalizing rates). The one place they break is fiscal crisis — banks are the transmission mechanism of a credit event, so the exact scenario your metals are built to hedge is the scenario financials get hurt in. That's not a bug; it's why the two sit together.
Defensives — wins / wins / flat / flat. The crisis insurance. They win in debasement (staples pass input-cost inflation through to consumers — an inflation hedge that pays you a dividend while you wait) and win in fiscal crisis (the classic flight-to-safety, stable cash flows). They go flat in the two growth scenarios because boring, stable businesses lag when the economy is booming. Notice this is the only proposed sleeve green in the crisis column — it pairs with financials to span the row.
Broad index — flat / lags / wins / wins. The benchmark. Flat in debasement (real returns muted), it actually lags in fiscal crisis (the index falls in a selloff), and wins in both AI upside and benign (it rides productivity and compounds in calm). It's the "own the market" ballast and the scoreboard you measure the rest against.
The single takeaway when you read it as a grid: your current book (top three) is a wall of green on the left and red/amber on the right — magnificent in the monetary scenarios, empty in the other two, which together carry ~45% of your own probability weight. The proposed core flips that. And the one column that stays hardest to cover is fiscal crisis — only your existing royalties plus defensives are green there, which is precisely why defensives earn their place and why a conviction-sized financials sleeve can never be allowed to crowd out the metals that are your crisis hedge.
